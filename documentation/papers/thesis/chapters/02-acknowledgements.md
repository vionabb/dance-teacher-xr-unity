## 2 Acknowledgements

This work has been shaped by many people over a long and, at times, meandering path.

I am especially grateful to my advisor, Devin Balkcom, for his guidance, which began well before this thesis, dating back to my undergraduate work. He has been an exceptional mentor---supportive, thoughtful, and generous with both his time and trust. He gave me the freedom to explore a research path that often diverged from his own background, and he met that divergence not with resistance, but with curiosity and encouragement. I am particularly thankful for his kindness throughout this process, for believing in me even when I was less certain, and for helping me see this work through.

I am grateful to the other faculty on the Human Motion Project. David Kraemer brought steady optimism and an unwavering engagement with the project. Xia Zhou offered keen perspectives and kind nudges that sharpened the work and helped me become a better scientist.

I would like to thank the rest of my examining committee, SouYoung Jin, Alberto Quattrini Li, and Lorie Loeb, for their time, insight, and thoughtful feedback.

I am thankful for my collaborators on the Human Motion Project---Megan Hillis, Yeongji Lee, Qijia Shao, Sixuan Han, and Colman Leung---and my peers in the robotics community at Dartmouth, including Mingi Jeong, Amy Sniffen, and Samuel Lensgraf for the camaraderie, shared effort, and intellectual companionship that gave life to my doctorate journey.

Finally, I am grateful to my parents. I wouldn't be here without you.
